01The Grammar Nobody Teaches

These are the unstated conventions the rest of math is built on. Most “I can't do algebra” is actually “nobody told me what the notation meant.”

= means “both sides weigh the same.” It is a scale, not “answer goes here.”

Why
Every legal move in algebra is just “keep the scale level” — that one idea generates all of solving.
Trap
On a calculator = means “go.” In algebra it never does.

Term = chunk separated by + or −. Factor = chunk separated by ×. Coefficient = the number stuck to the front.

Why
Almost every rule cares which one you're looking at, and almost every mistake is treating a term like a factor.
When
Before applying any rule, ask: am I looking at terms or factors?
Ex.
In 3x2 + 5x, there are 2 terms. In 3x2, the factors are 3, x, and x.

A number touching a letter means multiply. 3x = 3 · x. 2(x+1) = 2 × (x+1).

Why
The × symbol looks exactly like the letter x, so mathematicians just stopped writing it.

PEMDAS — Parentheses, Exponents, Multiply/Divide, Add/Subtract.

Why
Multiplication is repeated addition, so it's the “bigger” operation and has to be collapsed into addition before you can add.
Trap
M and D are the same rank — do them left to right. Same for A and S. 8 ÷ 2 × 4 = 16, not 1.
Hook
Please Excuse My Dear Aunt Sally — but Mom & Dad are twins, and so are Aunt & Sally.

The minus sign has three different jobs: subtract (5 − 3), negative (−3), and opposite (−x).

Why
−x is not “a negative number” — it's “the opposite of whatever x is.” If x = −4, then −x = 4.
Trap
This one trips up more people than anything else on this sheet.

02Negatives

Think of −1 as an instruction: “flip to the other side of zero.” Everything here falls out of that.

Subtracting is adding the opposite. a − b = a + (−b).

Why
It lets you stop tracking two different operations and just add signed numbers all day.
When
Any time a string of +/− signs is confusing you, rewrite the whole thing as addition first.

Same signs: add and keep the sign. Different signs: subtract and take the sign of the bigger one.

Why
Same direction = piling up. Opposite directions = tug of war, and the stronger side wins.
Ex.
−7 + 3 = −4 (tug of war, 7 wins, 7−3=4, negative).

Negative × negative = positive. Negative × positive = negative.

Why
×(−1) means “flip across zero.” Flip twice and you're back where you started.
Hook
Two wrongs make a right — but only in multiplication.

Distributing a negative flips every sign inside. −(a − b) = −a + b.

Why
That minus out front is really a −1 multiplying each term separately.
Trap
Forgetting the second sign. −(x − 5) = −x + 5, not −x − 5.

−x2 and (−x)2 are different things.

Why
The exponent grabs tighter than the minus sign, so −32 = −(32) = −9, but (−3)2 = 9.
When
If you want the negative squared, the parentheses are not optional.

03Fractions

One idea unlocks this whole section: the bottom number is a NAME, not a quantity.

The denominator names the size of the piece. The numerator counts them.load-bearing

Why
3/4 is “three pieces, and the pieces are called fourths” — the 4 is a unit label, like “feet.”
When
Every other fraction rule below is a consequence of this. When stuck, come back here.

Multiply straight across. a/b × c/d = ac/bd.

Why
Multiplying fractions is taking a piece of a piece — half of a third is a sixth, so the pieces get smaller and the names multiply.
Ex.
1/2 × 1/3 = 1/6

Adding or subtracting needs a common denominator first.load-bearing

Why
You can't count pieces of different sizes — same reason 3 feet + 2 meters isn't 5 of anything until you convert.
Trap
2/3 + 1/3 = 3/3, not 3/6. You're counting thirds; the name doesn't change.

Dividing = multiply by the reciprocal (flip the second one).

Why
“How many halves fit into 3?” is asking how many times you multiply up — so you flip and multiply.
Ex.
3 ÷ 1/2 = 3 × 2/1 = 6
Hook
Keep, Change, Flip.

You can cancel factors. You can never cancel terms.load-bearing

Why
Canceling is secretly dividing top and bottom by the same thing, which only works if that thing is multiplying everything.
Trap
(x+2)/2 is NOT x. But 2x/2 IS x.

a/b = c/d → ad = bc (cross-multiply).

Why
It isn't a trick — it's just multiplying both sides by b and then by d, and watching things cancel.
When
Two fractions set equal to each other. Proportions, unit conversion, scaling recipes.

04Exponents

An exponent is a tally of how many copies got written down. Every rule here is just counting them.

xa · xb = xa+b — multiplying, so add the exponents.

Why
x2 · x3 writes down 2 x's then 3 x's. Now count them: 5.
Trap
Only works when the base is the same. x2 · y3 doesn't combine.

xa / xb = xa-b — dividing, so subtract.

Why
Each x on the bottom cancels one on top. You're just counting the leftovers.

(xa)b = xab — power of a power, so multiply.

Why
b groups of (a copies of x). That's a·b copies.

x0 = 1. Anything to the zero is 1.

Why
x3/x3 is obviously 1, and the subtract rule says it's x0. Both are true, so x0 = 1.
Trap
It's 1, not 0. The 1 is the multiplicative starting point, the way 0 is for addition.

x-a = 1/xa. A negative exponent means flip it, not “make it negative.”load-bearing

Why
Walk the pattern down: 8, 4, 2, 1, ½, ¼. Each step down divides by 2 — nothing ever turns negative.

x1/2 = √x. A fractional exponent is a root.

Why
x1/2 · x1/2 = x1 by the add rule — so x1/2 is the thing that gives x when multiplied by itself. That's the square root.

(ab)n = an bn, but (a+b)n is NOT an + bn.load-bearing

Why
Exponents distribute over multiplication only. (2+3)2 = 25, while 22 + 32 = 13.
Trap
This is the single most common algebra error there is. (a+b)2 = a2 + 2ab + b2 — FOIL it, don't guess.

05Distributing & Factoring

These are the same move run in opposite directions. Distributing takes parentheses apart; factoring puts them back.

a(b + c) = ab + ac

Why
3 bags each holding (2 apples + 1 pear) gives you 6 apples and 3 pears. The 3 reaches everything inside.
Trap
It has to reach every term. 2(x + 3) = 2x + 6, not 2x + 3.

FOIL — (a+b)(c+d) = ac + ad + bc + bd

Why
It's just distributing twice: every term on the left has to meet every term on the right.
When
Two binomials multiplied. For anything bigger than 2×2 terms, forget FOIL and just make sure everything meets everything.
Hook
First, Outside, Inside, Last.

Combine like terms only: same letter, same exponent.

Why
3x + 2x2 is like 3 feet + 2 square feet — different units, so it doesn't collapse.
Ex.
3x + 5x = 8x. But 3x + 5x2 stays as it is.

Factor out the greatest common factor: ab + ac = a(b + c)

Why
Distribution running backwards. You're pulling the shared piece out front.
When
First thing to try on any factoring problem, always.

Difference of squares: a2 − b2 = (a + b)(a − b)

Why
FOIL it back: the two middle terms are +ab and −ab, which cancel.
When
Two perfect squares with a minus between them. Recognize it on sight — it shows up constantly.

06Solving for x

You are unwrapping a package. Whatever was wrapped last comes off first.

Whatever you do to one side, do to the other.load-bearing

Why
The = is a balance scale. Change one pan without the other and it stops being true.

Undo operations in reverse PEMDAS order: A/S first, then M/D, then exponents, then parentheses.load-bearing

Why
Wrapping a gift you go box → paper → ribbon. Unwrapping goes ribbon → paper → box. Solving is unwrapping.
Ex.
3x + 5 = 20 → subtract 5 → 3x = 15 → divide by 3 → x = 5

To move something, do the opposite operation.

Why
+ and − undo each other; × and ÷ undo each other. That's the only reason it works.
Trap
You're not “moving it to the other side and flipping the sign” — you're doing the same thing to both sides and one side cancels. Same result, but the second story is the one you can rebuild when you forget.

Flip the inequality when you multiply or divide by a negative.

Why
Multiplying by a negative mirrors the number line, so bigger becomes smaller. 2 < 3, but −2 > −3.
Trap
Only for × and ÷ by a negative. Adding or subtracting a negative doesn't flip anything.

Substitute your answer back in and check.

Why
It's the only step that catches a sign error, and sign errors are what actually cost you points.
When
Always. Especially on a test.

07Roots

√a · √b = √(ab), but √(a+b) is NOT √a + √b.

Why
Roots are just exponents in a hat — they distribute over multiplication only, same as every exponent rule.
Ex.
√(9+16) = √25 = 5, but √9 + √16 = 3 + 4 = 7.

Simplify by pulling out perfect-square factors. √50 = √(25·2) = 5√2.

Why
The multiply rule run backwards: split off the part that has a clean root and walk it out from under the radical.

√(x2) = |x|

Why
The radical symbol means “the positive one.” √((−3)2) = √9 = 3, not −3.

08Percents, Decimals & Ratios

Percent means per hundred. 35% = 35/100 = 0.35.load-bearing

Why
“cent” = hundred, same root as century and cents-per-dollar. A percent is always a fraction wearing a hat.

Decimal → percent: move the point 2 places right. Percent → decimal: 2 places left.

Why
That's multiplying or dividing by 100, which is the entire definition of percent.

“x% of y” means multiply.

Why
“Of” is the English word for multiplication. It never means anything else.
Ex.
20% of 60 = 0.20 × 60 = 12

Percent change = (new − old) / old

Why
You're measuring the change against where you started, so the starting value always goes on the bottom.
Trap
Going 100 → 150 is +50%. Going 150 → 100 is −33%, not −50%. The denominator changed.

A ratio a : b is just the fraction a/b.

Why
Different notation, same object. If ratios feel hard, rewrite them as fractions and use the fraction rules.

09Word Problem Translation

Word problems are a translation exercise, not a math exercise. Convert to symbols first, then solve.

of = × · is / are / was = = · per / for each = ÷ · and / more than / sum = + · difference / less = −

Why
These are fixed. They never mean anything else, which makes them the one part of word problems you can do mechanically.

“5 less than x” is x − 5. Backwards from how you read it.load-bearing

Why
“Less than” and “more than” describe a change to the thing named after them, so that thing comes first.
Trap
This wording is chosen specifically to trip you up. Slow down every single time you see it.

Name the unknown out loud before you write anything. “Let x = the number of hours.”

Why
Half of word-problem failure is solving correctly for the wrong quantity. Writing the units down prevents it.
