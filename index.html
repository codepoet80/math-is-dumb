01The Grammar Nobody Teaches

These are the unstated conventions the rest of math is built on. Most “I can't do algebra” is actually “nobody told me what the notation meant.”

1.1= means “both sides weigh the same.” It is a scale, not “answer goes here.”

Why
Every legal move in algebra is just “keep the scale level” — that one idea generates all of solving.
Trap
On a calculator = means “go.” In algebra it never does.

1.2Term = chunk separated by + or −. Factor = chunk separated by ×. Coefficient = the number stuck to the front.

Why
Almost every rule cares which one you're looking at, and almost every mistake is treating a term like a factor.
When
Before applying any rule, ask: am I looking at terms or factors?
Ex.
In 3x2 + 5x, there are 2 terms. In 3x2, the factors are 3, x, and x.

1.3A number touching a letter means multiply. 3x = 3 · x. 2(x+1) = 2 × (x+1).

Why
The × symbol looks exactly like the letter x, so mathematicians just stopped writing it.

1.4PEMDAS — Parentheses, Exponents, Multiply/Divide, Add/Subtract.

Why
Multiplication is repeated addition, so it's the “bigger” operation and has to be collapsed into addition before you can add.
Trap
M and D are the same rank — do them left to right. Same for A and S. 8 ÷ 2 × 4 = 16, not 1.
Hook
Please Excuse My Dear Aunt Sally — but Mom & Dad are twins, and so are Aunt & Sally.

1.5The minus sign has three different jobs: subtract (5 − 3), negative (−3), and opposite (−x).

Why
−x is not “a negative number” — it's “the opposite of whatever x is.” If x = −4, then −x = 4.
Trap
This one trips up more people than anything else on this sheet.

1.6“Simplify” is not an operation. It means “write this in the shortest standard form,” and which moves that takes depends on what you're holding.

Why
Expanding and factoring are moves. Simplifying is a destination. That's why the same word can mean opposite moves on different problems.
When
First ask what kind of thing is this? — the type picks the tool.
Trap
For a polynomial, simplified means expanded. For a fraction, simplified means factored. Same word, opposite directions.
Ex.
Brackets → expand, combine like terms, highest power first (05). Fraction → factor, cancel shared factors (03). Root → pull out perfect squares (07). Exponents → apply the rules until each base appears once (04).

02Negatives

Think of −1 as an instruction: “flip to the other side of zero.” Everything here falls out of that.

2.1Subtracting is adding the opposite. a − b = a + (−b).

Why
It lets you stop tracking two different operations and just add signed numbers all day.
When
Any time a string of +/− signs is confusing you, rewrite the whole thing as addition first.

2.2Same signs: add and keep the sign. Different signs: subtract and take the sign of the bigger one.

Why
Same direction = piling up. Opposite directions = tug of war, and the stronger side wins.
Ex.
−7 + 3 = −4 (tug of war, 7 wins, 7−3=4, negative).

2.3Negative × negative = positive. Negative × positive = negative.

Why
×(−1) means “flip across zero.” Flip twice and you're back where you started.
Hook
Two wrongs make a right — but only in multiplication.

2.4Distributing a negative flips every sign inside. −(a − b) = −a + b.

Why
That minus out front is really a −1 multiplying each term separately.
Trap
Forgetting the second sign. −(x − 5) = −x + 5, not −x − 5.

2.5−x2 and (−x)2 are different things.

Why
The exponent grabs tighter than the minus sign, so −32 = −(32) = −9, but (−3)2 = 9.
When
If you want the negative squared, the parentheses are not optional.

03Fractions

One idea unlocks this whole section: the bottom number is a NAME, not a quantity.

3.1The denominator names the size of the piece. The numerator counts them.load-bearing

Why
34 is “three pieces, and the pieces are called fourths” — the 4 is a unit label, like “feet.”
When
Every other fraction rule below is a consequence of this. When stuck, come back here.

3.2Multiply straight across. ab × cd = acbd.

Why
Multiplying fractions is taking a piece of a piece — half of a third is a sixth, so the pieces get smaller and the names multiply.
Trap
A whole number sits over an invisible 1, so it only multiplies the top. a · bc = a1 · bc = abc, not abac.
Ex.
12 × 13 = 16

3.3Adding or subtracting needs a common denominator first.load-bearing

Why
You can't count pieces of different sizes — same reason 3 feet + 2 meters isn't 5 of anything until you convert.
Trap
23 + 13 = 33, not 36. You're counting thirds; the name doesn't change.

3.4Dividing = multiply by the reciprocal (flip the second one).

Why
“How many halves fit into 3?” is asking how many times you multiply up — so you flip and multiply.
Ex.
3 ÷ 12 = 3 × 21 = 6
Hook
Keep, Change, Flip.

3.51ab = ba — one over a fraction is that fraction flipped upside down.

Why
It's Keep, Change, Flip with a 1 on top: 1 ÷ ab = 1 × ba, and multiplying by 1 changes nothing.
Ex.
123 = 32. Check: 32 · 23 = 1.

3.6You can cancel factors. You can never cancel terms.load-bearing

Why
Canceling is secretly dividing top and bottom by the same thing, which only works if that thing is multiplying everything.
Trap
x+22 is NOT x. But 2x2 IS x.

3.7ab = cd → ad = bc (cross-multiply).

Why
It isn't a trick — it's just multiplying both sides by b and then by d, and watching things cancel.
When
Two fractions set equal to each other. Proportions, unit conversion, scaling recipes.

04Exponents

An exponent is a tally of how many copies got written down. Every rule here is just counting them.

4.1xa · xb = xa+b — multiplying, so add the exponents.

Why
x2 · x3 writes down 2 x's then 3 x's. Now count them: 5.
Trap
Only works when the base is the same. x2 · y3 doesn't combine.

4.2xaxb = xa-b — dividing, so subtract.load-bearing

Why
Each x on the bottom cancels one on top. You're just counting the leftovers.
Ex.
v2/7v3/5 = v2/7 − 3/5. Those are fractions being subtracted, so they need a common denominator (03): 1035 − 2135 = −1135, giving v−11/35.

4.3(xa)b = xab — power of a power, so multiply.

Why
b groups of (a copies of x). That's a·b copies.

4.4x0 = 1. Anything to the zero is 1.

Why
x3x3 is obviously 1, and the subtract rule says it's x0. Both are true, so x0 = 1.
Trap
It's 1, not 0. The 1 is the multiplicative starting point, the way 0 is for addition.

4.5x-a = 1xa, and 1x-a = xa. A negative exponent means it wants to be on the other side of the fraction bar. Move it across and the minus goes away.load-bearing

Why
xa is “multiply by x, a times,” so x-a is “divide by x, a times” — that lives on the bottom. Walk the pattern down: 8, 4, 2, 1, ½, ¼. Nothing ever turns negative.
Trap
It's one move, not two rules. Top or bottom, cross the bar and drop the sign. No fraction bar means everything is on top, over an invisible 1 — the bottom is empty until something crosses down into it. −5z-5 = −5z-51 = −5z5.

4.6x1/2 = √x, x1/3 = 3√x. A fractional exponent is a root.

Why
x1/2 · x1/2 = x1 by the add rule — so x1/2 is the thing that gives x when multiplied by itself. That's the square root. Three copies of x1/3 make x1, so it's the cube root.

4.7xm/n = (n√x)m. Bottom of the fraction is the root, top is the power.

Why
xm/n = (x1/n)m by the multiply rule, and x1/n is the nth root for the same reason x1/2 is the square root.
Ex.
82/3 = (3√8)2 = 22 = 4. Take the root first so the numbers stay small.

4.8x-m/n = 1(n√x)m. Three jobs, read off the exponent: the minus flips, the bottom is the root, the top is the power.

Why
Each part is a rule you already have: the minus sends it across the fraction bar, then xm/n is root-then-power. The minus has nothing to do with the root or the power.
Trap
A negative exponent never makes the answer negative. 125-2/3 is a small positive number, not −25.
Ex.
125-2/3 = 11252/3 = 1(3√125)2 = 152 = 125.

4.92m2 = 2 · (m2), not (2m)2. An exponent grabs only the one thing it touches.

Why
Exponents beat multiplication in PEMDAS, so the 2 is just standing next to m2. Plug in m = 3: 2m2 is 18, but (2m)2 is 36.
Trap
Same with negatives. 2m-2 = 2m2 — only the m crosses the bar. If you want the whole 2m raised, the parentheses are not optional.

4.10(ab)n = anbn. A power on a fraction goes to the top and the bottom.

Why
(ab)3 = ab · ab · ab, and fractions multiply straight across: a·a·ab·b·b.
Ex.
(18)4/3 = 14/384/3 = 1(3√8)4 = 124 = 116. 1 to any power is 1, so the top just stays 1.

4.11(ab)-n = (ba)n. A negative power on a whole fraction: flip the fraction and drop the minus.

Why
The minus sends the top down and the bottom up, all at once. That's the same as flipping the fraction.
Ex.
(2x23x6)-3 = (3x62x2)3. Flip it first so every exponent after that is positive.

4.12(ab)n = an bn, but (a+b)n is NOT an + bn.load-bearing

Why
Exponents distribute over multiplication only. (2+3)2 = 25, while 22 + 32 = 13.
Trap
This is the single most common algebra error there is. (a+b)2 = a2 + 2ab + b2 — FOIL it, don't guess.
Ex.
(3x4)3 = 33 · (x4)3 = 27x12, not 3x12. The coefficient is a factor too, so it gets cubed.

05Distributing & Factoring

These are the same move run in opposite directions. Distributing takes parentheses apart; factoring puts them back.

5.1Factoring = rewriting a sum as a multiplication. That's the whole definition. Length has nothing to do with it.load-bearing

Why
A product is something you can grab: it's zero only when one factor is zero (13), and you can cancel factors (03). A sum gives you neither.
When
Ask: is the top level of this expression things added or things multiplied? Factoring turns the first into the second.
Trap
It is not “writing it out long.” x2 − 1 → (x − 1)(x + 1) gets longer; x2 + 2x + 1 → (x + 1)2 gets shorter. Both are factoring.

5.2a(b + c) = ab + ac

Why
3 bags each holding (2 apples + 1 pear) gives you 6 apples and 3 pears. The 3 reaches everything inside.
Trap
It has to reach every term. 2(x + 3) = 2x + 6, not 2x + 3.

5.3FOIL — (a+b)(c+d) = ac + ad + bc + bd

Why
It's just distributing twice: every term on the left has to meet every term on the right.
When
Two binomials multiplied. For anything bigger than 2×2 terms, forget FOIL and just make sure everything meets everything.
Hook
First, Outside, Inside, Last.

5.4Combine like terms only: same letter, same exponent.

Why
3x + 2x2 is like 3 feet + 2 square feet — different units, so it doesn't collapse.
Ex.
3x + 5x = 8x. But 3x + 5x2 stays as it is.

5.5Factor out the greatest common factor: ab + ac = a(b + c)

Why
Distribution running backwards. You're pulling the shared piece out front.
When
First thing to try on any factoring problem, always.

5.6Difference of squares: a2 − b2 = (a − b)(a + b). Take the root of each term; one bracket subtracts them, the other adds them.

Why
FOIL it back: the middle terms are +ab and −ab, which cancel, and (−b)(+b) is what makes b2 come out negative. Only the mixed-sign pair does both.
When
Two perfect squares with a minus between them. Recognize it on sight — it shows up constantly.
Trap
Only a difference. a2 + b2 doesn't factor — with two plus signs the middle terms can't cancel.
Ex.
9y2 − 25x2: roots are 3y and 5x, so it's (3y − 5x)(3y + 5x). FOIL it to check: the 15xy terms cancel.

06Solving for x

You are unwrapping a package. Whatever was wrapped last comes off first.

6.1Whatever you do to one side, do to the other.load-bearing

Why
The = is a balance scale. Change one pan without the other and it stops being true.

6.2Undo operations in reverse PEMDAS order: A/S first, then M/D, then exponents, then parentheses.load-bearing

Why
Wrapping a gift you go box → paper → ribbon. Unwrapping goes ribbon → paper → box. Solving is unwrapping.
Ex.
3x + 5 = 20 → subtract 5 → 3x = 15 → divide by 3 → x = 5

6.3Fractions in an equation? Either multiply every term on both sides by the LCD first — or keep the fractions, get x down to one fraction coefficient, and multiply both sides by its flip.load-bearing

Why
Both are “do the same thing to both sides.” The LCD deletes every denominator in one move; the flip undoes a fraction the same way ÷ undoes × (03).
Trap
LCD route: multiply every term, including the ones with no fraction. Forgetting the lone 7 is the classic error.
Ex.
x3 + x4 = 7. LCD: ×12 everywhere → 4x + 3x = 84 → x = 12. Flip: combine → (712)x = 7 → ×127 both sides → x = 12.

6.4To move something, do the opposite operation.

Why
+ and − undo each other; × and ÷ undo each other. That's the only reason it works.
Trap
You're not “moving it to the other side and flipping the sign” — you're doing the same thing to both sides and one side cancels. Same result, but the second story is the one you can rebuild when you forget.

6.5Flip the inequality when you multiply or divide by a negative.

Why
Multiplying by a negative mirrors the number line, so bigger becomes smaller. 2 < 3, but −2 > −3.
Trap
Only for × and ÷ by a negative. Adding or subtracting a negative doesn't flip anything.

6.6Substitute your answer back in and check.

Why
It's the only step that catches a sign error, and sign errors are what actually cost you points.
When
Always. Especially on a test.

07Roots

7.1√a · √b = √ab, but √a+b is NOT √a + √b.

Why
Roots are just exponents in a hat — they distribute over multiplication only, same as every exponent rule.
Ex.
√9+16 = √25 = 5, but √9 + √16 = 3 + 4 = 7.

7.2Simplify by pulling out perfect-square factors. √50 = √25·2 = 5√2.

Why
The multiply rule run backwards: split off the part that has a clean root and walk it out from under the radical.

7.3√x8 = x4: an even exponent gets halved. An odd exponent leaves one copy inside: √y5 = √y4 · y = y2√y.

Why
√ is the 12 power (04), so it halves the exponent. Halving only works cleanly on even numbers, so split off the biggest even part and leave one copy under the radical.
Trap
These problems assume the variables are positive, which is why no |x| shows up in the answer (see √x2 = |x| below).
Ex.
√75x8y5 = √25·3 · x8 · y4·y = 5x4y2√3y. Everything that halves comes out, and the leftovers stay inside together.

7.4a√n + b√n = (a+b)√n. Same root: add the numbers in front. Different roots: simplify each one first, because they often turn out to match.

Why
√3 is a unit, like x. 2√3 + 5√3 is 2 of them plus 5 of them, the same as combining like terms (05).
Trap
√a + √b is NOT √a+b, so √2 + √3 stays as it is. With no shared root after simplifying, there's nothing to combine.
Ex.
√75 + 5√27 = 5√3 + 5·3√3 = 5√3 + 15√3 = 20√3.

7.5√x2 = |x|

Why
The radical symbol means “the positive one.” √(−3)2 = √9 = 3, not −3.

08Percents, Decimals & Ratios

8.1Percent means per hundred. 35% = 35100 = 0.35.load-bearing

Why
“cent” = hundred, same root as century and cents-per-dollar. A percent is always a fraction wearing a hat.

8.2Decimal → percent: move the point 2 places right. Percent → decimal: 2 places left.

Why
That's multiplying or dividing by 100, which is the entire definition of percent.

8.3“x% of y” means multiply.

Why
“Of” is the English word for multiplication. It never means anything else.
Ex.
20% of 60 = 0.20 × 60 = 12

8.4Percent change = new − oldold

Why
You're measuring the change against where you started, so the starting value always goes on the bottom.
Trap
Going 100 → 150 is +50%. Going 150 → 100 is −33%, not −50%. The denominator changed.

8.5A ratio a : b is just the fraction ab.

Why
Different notation, same object. If ratios feel hard, rewrite them as fractions and use the fraction rules.

09Functions & f(x) Notation

A function is a machine: put a number in, get exactly one number out. Nearly all the difficulty here is notation, not concept.

9.1f(x) does not mean f times x. It means "the machine f, applied to x."load-bearing

Why
This is the one place in algebra where parentheses don't mean multiply — they mean "fed into."
Trap
You spent all of section 05 learning that 2(x+1) means multiply. f(x+1) does not. The rule changed and nobody said so.

9.2f(x) and y are the same thing.

Why
Both name the output. y = 2x + 1 and f(x) = 2x + 1 are identical; the f version just tells you which machine made it.
When
When a problem switches between them mid-question, nothing has changed. Keep going.

9.3To find f(3), replace every x with 3.

Why
x is a blank to be filled, not a value. The function is a recipe with a hole in it.
Ex.
If f(x) = x2 + 1, then f(3) = 32 + 1 = 10.

9.4f(x+1) means put x+1 into every blank. It is not f(x) + 1.load-bearing

Why
Whatever sits in the parentheses is the input, however complicated it looks.
Trap
If f(x) = x2, then f(x+1) = (x+1)2, not x2 + 1.

9.5Domain = what you're allowed to put in. Range = what can come out.

Why
Only two things ever break a function at this level: dividing by zero, and even roots of negatives. Check for those and the domain is everything else.
Hook
Domain is the Door (in). Range is the Result (out).

9.6A graph is a function if no vertical line crosses it twice.

Why
One input must give exactly one output — two crossings means one x produced two y's, so it isn't a machine.

10The Coordinate Plane & Straight Lines

This section pays off later: a regression line in statistics is exactly y = mx + b wearing different letters.

10.1A point is (x, y) — always across first, then up.

Why
Alphabetical order, x before y. That's the whole reason; there isn't a deeper one.
Hook
Into the house, then up the stairs.

10.2Slope m = riserun = y2 - y1x2 - x1load-bearing

Why
Slope is a rate: how much y changes for every 1 that x moves. Every other line rule follows from that one sentence.
Trap
Keep the points in the same order top and bottom. If y2 came first, x2 comes first too.

10.3y = mx + b — m is the slope, b is where the line crosses the y-axis.load-bearing

Why
Set x to 0: the mx term vanishes and you're left with y = b. So b is literally the value at the crossing.

10.4Positive slope climbs left-to-right. Negative falls. Zero is flat. Vertical is undefined.

Why
It's rise over run: no rise gives 0, and no run gives division by zero, which isn't a number.
Trap
Zero slope and undefined slope are opposites, not synonyms. Flat line: m = 0. Vertical line: no m at all.

10.5Parallel lines have the same slope. Perpendicular slopes are negative reciprocals.

Why
Turning a line 90° swaps rise and run and flips the direction — so 23 becomes -32.
Ex.
Perpendicular to m = 2 is m = -12.

10.6To graph it: plot b first, then count the slope as rise-over-run to get the next point.

Why
b hands you one free point, and the slope is a set of directions from any point to another.

10.7y-intercept: set x = 0. x-intercept: set y = 0.

Why
An intercept is where the other coordinate is zero — that's what touching an axis means.

11Systems of Equations

Two equations, two unknowns. The answer is the one point that satisfies both — which on a graph is where the lines cross.

11.1A solution must make both equations true at once.load-bearing

Why
That's why the answer is the crossing point: it's the only place that sits on both lines.

11.2Substitution: solve one equation for one variable, then put that into the other.

Why
It converts a two-unknown problem into a one-unknown problem, which you already know how to solve.
When
Best when a variable is already alone, or has a coefficient of 1.

11.3Elimination: add or subtract the two equations so one variable cancels.

Why
You're allowed to add equals to equals — both scales stay balanced, so the combined statement is still true.
When
Best when the same variable has matching or opposite coefficients. Multiply one equation through to make that happen.

11.4Variables all vanish? A false statement (0 = 5) means no solution. A true one (0 = 0) means infinitely many.

Why
No solution means the lines are parallel and never meet. Infinite means they're the same line drawn twice.
Trap
Getting 0 = 0 feels like you did something wrong. You didn't — it's an answer.

12Inequalities & Absolute Value

Absolute value means distance from zero. Every rule here is that one idea applied to a number line.

12.1|x| is the distance from zero, so it's never negative.load-bearing

Why
Distance has no direction. Going 5 left and 5 right are both a trip of 5.

12.2|x| = 5 has two answers: 5 and -5.load-bearing

Why
Both sit 5 units from zero, and the bars threw away which side you were on.
Trap
Losing the negative answer is the single most common absolute-value mistake.

12.3|x| < 5 means -5 < x < 5 (a band). |x| > 5 means x < -5 or x > 5 (two pieces).load-bearing

Why
"Less than 5 away from zero" is the stretch around zero. "More than 5 away" is everything outside it.
Hook
Less thAND, greatOR.

12.4Get the absolute value alone before you split it into two cases.

Why
The bars apply to whatever is inside them, so anything still stuck outside hasn't been dealt with yet.
Ex.
2|x| + 1 = 11 becomes |x| = 5 first, then x = 5 or x = -5.

12.5Graphing: open circle for < and >, filled circle for <= and >=.

Why
Filled means the endpoint itself counts. Open means "up to here but not this."

12.6Multiplying or dividing an inequality by a negative still flips it — see 06.

Why
Same reason as before: multiplying by a negative mirrors the number line, so bigger becomes smaller.
When
It applies inside absolute-value cases too, and that's where people forget it.

13Quadratics

Anything with an x2. It graphs as a parabola — a U — because squaring turns negatives positive, so the curve has to turn around.

13.1Zero Product Property: if A · B = 0, then A = 0 or B = 0.load-bearing

Why
The only way to multiply to zero is for something to already be zero. This is the entire reason factoring solves equations.
Trap
It only works against zero. A · B = 6 tells you nothing about A or B — get everything to one side first.

13.2To factor x2 + bx + c: find two numbers that multiply to c and add to b.load-bearing

Why
It's FOIL backwards — the Last terms multiply to c, and the Outside plus Inside add to b.
Ex.
x2 + 5x + 6: 2 and 3 multiply to 6, add to 5. So (x+2)(x+3).

13.3Quadratic formula: x = -b ± √b2 - 4ac2a

Why
It's completing the square done once, in general, so nobody ever has to do it by hand again.
When
When factoring doesn't work — which is most of the time outside a textbook.
Trap
The whole -b ± √b2 - 4ac sits over 2a — the -b included, not just the root.

13.4± means work it twice: once with +, once with -.

Why
A square root has two answers, so the formula hands you both at once.

13.5The discriminant b2 - 4ac counts the real answers: positive → 2, zero → 1, negative → 0.

Why
It lives under the square root. A negative under a root has no real answer, so the parabola never crosses the x-axis.
When
Useful when a question only asks how many solutions — you can skip the rest of the formula.

13.6Get it to ax2 + bx + c = 0 before doing anything else.

Why
Both factoring and the formula assume one side is zero. Neither works otherwise.

14Rational Expressions

Fractions with letters in them. There are no new rules here — section 03 already covers all of it. The only new skill is factoring first.

14.1Every fraction rule from 03 applies unchanged. Letters change nothing.

Why
A fraction is a fraction. xy · ab multiplies straight across for exactly the same reason 12 · 13 does.

14.2Factor first, then cancel. Never cancel before factoring.load-bearing

Why
You can only cancel factors, never terms — and factoring is the operation that turns terms into factors.
Ex.
x2 - 4x + 2 → (x+2)(x-2)x+2 → x - 2.

14.3The denominator can never be zero. Note which x values are banned.

Why
Division by zero is undefined, so those inputs are removed from the domain — even if they cancel away later.
Trap
In the example above, x = -2 stays banned even though the (x+2) cancelled. The cancelling doesn't un-ban it.

14.4Adding or subtracting still needs a common denominator — factor to find it.

Why
Same reason as numbers: you can't count pieces of different sizes. The factored form shows you what's missing.

15Scientific Notation

Short section, but you will need it the moment you meet a p-value.

15.1a × 10n, where a is at least 1 and less than 10.

Why
Forcing exactly one digit before the decimal makes any two numbers instantly comparable by their exponent.

15.2Positive exponent → big number. Negative exponent → small number.

Why
The exponent counts how many places the decimal moves, and negative exponents mean divide — see 04.
Ex.
1.2 × 10-5 is 0.000012. That's a very small p-value.

15.3Move the decimal n places: right for positive n, left for negative.

Why
Multiplying by 10 shifts every digit one column up. Doing it n times shifts n columns.

16Word Problem Translation

Word problems are a translation exercise, not a math exercise. Convert to symbols first, then solve.

16.1of = × · is / are / was = = · per / for each = ÷ · and / more than / sum = + · difference / less = −

Why
These are fixed. They never mean anything else, which makes them the one part of word problems you can do mechanically.

16.2“5 less than x” is x − 5. Backwards from how you read it.load-bearing

Why
“Less than” and “more than” describe a change to the thing named after them, so that thing comes first.
Trap
This wording is chosen specifically to trip you up. Slow down every single time you see it.

16.3Name the unknown out loud before you write anything. “Let x = the number of hours.”

Why
Half of word-problem failure is solving correctly for the wrong quantity. Writing the units down prevents it.
